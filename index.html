Port and service scanning by the SOCRadar Attack Surface Management Platform

What is the Curiosity Wide Scan Project?

Curiosity Wide Scan is the scanning subproject of the SOCRadar platform. It continuously looks for open ports and service banners on our customers’ external IP addresses. When a scan finds an unexpected open port, an exposed service or a likely misconfiguration, the platform raises an alarm so the customer can fix it before an attacker finds it.

Two groups of scanners take part in the project:

Scanner group What it does
Port scannersSend TCP SYN probes to discover open ports and collect service banners.
Network vulnerability scannersCheck the services found on open ports for known vulnerabilities and misconfigurations.

The IP addresses of both groups are listed at the bottom of this page.

How we scan

The scanning system is built to go unnoticed by the hosts it probes:

  • Low rate. Port discovery uses TCP SYN packets sent at well under 0.1 Mbps.

  • Distributed. Scans are spread across the scanner addresses listed below rather than sent from a single source.

  • Randomized over time. Probes are spread out in time rather than sent in bursts.

  • No load by design. Together these measures keep the scans from placing any load on the destination addresses.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I see connections from SOCRadar addresses in my logs?

One of our scanners probed your IP address as part of the Curiosity Wide Scan Project. The scan looks for open ports, service banners and potential misconfigurations, at the low, randomized rate described above. If your organization is a SOCRadar customer, any finding appears as an alarm in your platform.

Is the scanning harmful or disruptive?

It is designed not to be. Probes stay below 0.1 Mbps and are distributed across addresses and randomized over time, so they place no load on your hosts.

How can I be excluded from the scans?

Email noscan@socradar.io and ask to be excluded. Include your organization name, the IP addresses or ranges to exclude and a contact person so we can confirm the request.

How can I whitelist the scanners?

Add the IP addresses listed in the next section to the whitelist (allow list) of your firewall, IDS/IPS and WAF. This lets our scans complete, so the findings reflect your real exposure instead of what your perimeter happens to let through. If a new address is added to the list or an existing one changes, a notification is issued through the SOCRadar platform and this page is revised accordingly.

Who can I contact with questions?

Write to noscan@socradar.io.
